The History of Independence Day In India

Independence Day, in India, is a national holiday celebrated annually on August 15. Independence Day marks the end of the British Raj in 1947, brought about by the Indian Independence Act of July 18 that year, and the establishment of a free and independent Indian nation following the long struggle of the Indian Independence Movement. It also marks the anniversary of the partition of India into two countries, India and Pakistan, which occurred at midnight on August 14/15, 1947. India celebrates Independence Day on August 15, whereas Pakistan observes it on August 14, in recognition of the date on which independence was formally declared there.


The Freedom Fighters of India

Mahatma Gandhi mobilized millions of citizens through the power of non - violent resistance. He led historic nationwide campaigns like the Salt March to systematically dismantle British moral authority. Mahatma Gandhi led the 1942 Quit India movement demanding an end to British rule. Gandhi weaponized non-violence through Satyagraha, using hunger strikes, mass boycotts, and peaceful non-cooperation to paralyze the British administration without firing a single shot. He was assassinated on January 30, 1948, after achieving Indian independence.



In contrast, Subhas Chandra Bose took a militant approach to liberation. He traveled abroad to build the formidable Indian National Army to challenge colonial rule through direct armed conflict. Subhas Chandra Bose envisioned a modern, industrialized independent nation and advocated for rapid economic planning as the president of the Indian National Congress. He reportedly sustained third-degree burns and died in a military hospital in Taipei following a plane crash on August 18, 1945.



The fiery youth icon Bhagat Singh shook the British administration through calculated acts of revolutionary defiance. His supreme martyrdom at a young age galvanized the entire nation to fight for independence. He fearlessly popularized the slogan Inquilab Zindabad from inside prison walls to ignite a militant spirit among the youth. He was executed by hanging on March 23, 1931, at the Lahore Central Jail when he was only twenty-three years old.
